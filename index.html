Dr. Sayan Atta

Postdoctoral Scholar · Bioinorganic Chemistry & Chemical Biology

Pluth Research Group, University of Oregon

I am a postdoctoral scholar in the Pluth Lab at the University of Oregon. I study the fundamental chemistry of reactive sulfur and selenium species and how they interact with biologically active metal centers. My current work focuses on persulfides and their reactions with reactive nitrogen species, including reactions that generate nitric oxide.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemistry with Prof. Amit Majumdar at the Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science (IACS), Kolkata. My doctoral research focused on transition-metal complexes of polychalcogenides and nitrite, particularly sulfur and selenium reactivity and the reduction of nitrite to nitric oxide. Before joining IACS, I completed my M.Sc. at IIT Guwahati and worked there as a project assistant with Prof. Tharmalingam Punniyamurthy.

Research

Persulfides and nitric oxide chemistry

I study the reactions of metal-bound persulfides with reactive nitrogen species. In our recent preprint, we examined a synthetic Zn²⁺ persulfide that releases nitric oxide through transnitrosation and nitrite reduction. The nitrite reaction requires activation by a Brønsted or Lewis acid.

Preprint

Diiron complexes and nitrite reduction

At IACS, I synthesized and studied nonheme diiron(II) complexes that reduce nitrite to nitric oxide, including functional models relevant to the protein YtfE. I examined how coordination environment, redox properties, and nitric oxide binding affect their reactivity and catalysis.

Link · Link

Sulfur and selenium ligands in metal complexes

My doctoral work also explored the synthesis and reactivity of metal complexes with sulfur- and selenium-based ligands. This included a redox-convergent route to a cobalt(III)–pentasulfido complex and studies of zinc complexes reacting with dioxygen and nitrite to form oxidized chalcogen products and reactive sulfur and selenium species.

Link · Link

Experimental methods

I use glovebox and Schlenk-line techniques to prepare and handle air-sensitive compounds. I combine crystallography, spectroscopy, and electrochemistry to determine their structures and study their reactions. My experience includes single-crystal X-ray diffraction, multinuclear NMR, UV–Vis, FTIR, and EPR spectroscopy, as well as cyclic voltammetry and bulk electrolysis.

Mentoring & research training

I have mentored graduate, project, and rotation students through my research at IACS and the University of Oregon. I have also provided hands-on training in single-crystal X-ray diffraction and GC–MS.

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction

IACS · 2021–2025

As the student representative for single-crystal XRD, I trained graduate students and postdoctoral researchers in data collection and structure refinement using a Bruker D8 VENTURE Microfocus diffractometer. My crystallography work has contributed to more than 40 published molecular structures.

Gas chromatography and mass spectrometry

IACS · 2024–2025

I trained graduate students and postdoctoral researchers in GC–MS methods for liquid samples and headspace gas mixtures. My gas chromatography experience also includes thermal conductivity and flame ionization detection using a Thermo Fisher Trace 1300 system.
